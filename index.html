How It Works

Team pickleball, the way the colleges play it

Matches use the coed collegiate team format — five events, each worth one team point. First team to three points wins, but all five events are usually played to maximize participation.

2 Girls + 2 Boys

That's all it takes to field a team. Rosters carry up to 6 players in the regular season and up to 8 in tournament play — enrolled students, grades 9–12, certified by their school. Schools may field more than one team until the Club Championships begin; from there, one team of up to 8 players through the State Finals.

Five Events Per Match

Girls Doubles, Boys Doubles, Mixed Doubles #1 and #2 — then, when it's tied 2–2, the HeartBreaker: a rotating singles shoot-out tie-breaker that is the most exciting finish in team pickleball.

Zero Conflict With School Sports

The season starts after every IHSAA winter sport begins and ends before the first spring sport starts. Your basketball, swimming, and wrestling programs never compete for athletes.

The Press Box

Real coverage. Student bylines.

The Press Box belongs to Indiana's student journalists. High school newspaper departments (or school-approved student writers) file short reads, and TV sports departments (or school-approved videographers) file short videos — match highlights, interviews, or your own creative story lines. One rule for everything: the one-minute rule — about a one-minute read, about a one-minute video. Every submission is reviewed before it's published, the wall holds the ten freshest pieces, and it turns over every few days — so keep filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Good questions. Straight answers.

What is the Indiana High School Team Pickleball Club League?

It is the first Indiana statewide league for high school pickleball clubs. Schools field teams of boys and girls who play other schools' teams at a regional indoor Home Club, mostly on weekends, from late November through mid-January, followed by Regional Club Championships and the road to the State Championships, which end on Championship Saturday, February 13, 2027, at Drew Brees Championship Court, Picklr Noblesville.

Which schools can take part?

IIPA is inviting 412 Indiana high schools, the IHSAA member and provisional member schools that field interscholastic sports teams. A school does not need an existing pickleball club to register; registering is a fine way to start one.

Is this an IHSAA sport?

No. IIPA is an independent nonprofit, and the league is a club activity organized outside the IHSAA calendar, during the window between winter and spring sports. Schools take part the way they would in any club activity.

What does a team look like?

A team is at least two boys and two girls. A match has five events: Girls Doubles, Boys Doubles, Mixed Doubles 1, Mixed Doubles 2, and a deciding fifth event called the HeartBreaker. A school may bring up to eight players to a match and may enter more than one team.

Where are matches played?

At one of 15 Regional Home Clubs, indoor pickleball facilities across Indiana and into Ohio. Each school is assigned to the Home Club for its area. Matches are played indoors, on everyday courts, mostly on weekends.

When is the season?

The regular season runs ten weekends, starting November 21, 2026, with a break over the holidays, and ending January 30, 2027. Regional Club Championships are played in the final weeks of the regular season. The State Championships are held in the Indianapolis area over the first two weekends of February, ending on Championship Saturday, February 13, 2027, at Drew Brees Championship Court, Picklr Noblesville.

What does it cost a school?

$180 for a school's first team for the season, and $60 for each additional team. That fee covers the league itself: organization, schedules, standings and score posting, the rulebook, medals, the Regional Club Championships, and the road to the State Championships. Court time and anything else a Home Club offers are arranged between the school and its Home Club; IIPA is not involved in those arrangements.

How does a school register?

Click “Register Your School” on this site, choose your school, and pay the team fee. Registration closes October 30, 2026. Your first team is automatically named for your school and mascot (for example, Rushville Lions); additional teams are numbered (Rushville 2, Rushville 3, and so on).

Who runs the club at the school?

Each school names a Pickleball Club Sponsor, a teacher or other adult approved by the school, who is IIPA's main contact for the club. Teams may be coached by the sponsor or by any school-approved adult age 21 or older who knows the game.

Do players need experience or their own equipment?

No experience is required; many players will be new to the game. Players need a paddle and court shoes, and IIPA's Pro Shop page will list partner discounts for students. Schools that need help with equipment or fees can apply to the Every Kid Plays Fund.

Are there tryouts or eligibility rules?

Any student at a high school can be in that school's pickleball club, subject to the school's own rules. The club sponsor and coaches decide who plays in matches based on their own criteria. IIPA does not decide who is on a club or who plays; players must be enrolled students at the school they play for.

What about waivers, insurance, and safety?

Every player and a parent or guardian sign IIPA's participation agreement and liability waiver online before the first match. IIPA carries liability insurance for its events, follows Indiana's concussion protocol, and each Home Club has an emergency action plan. Students remain under the supervision of their school's adult at all times.

How do standings and championships work?

Every match result is posted on this site, and each region's standings update automatically. The top finishers in each region advance to the State Championships, where the 48 schools that qualify through the 15 Regional Clubs play through two weekends, and the last two teams standing meet on Championship Saturday, at Drew Brees Championship Court, Picklr Noblesville.

Can a school have more than one team?

Yes. A school may enter as many teams as it likes during the regular season and can change lineups from week to week from its club roster. Before championship play, each school names one eight-player Championship Roster for its mascot team; that roster represents the school in the Regional Club Championships and the State Championships.

Can students who don't play still be involved?

Yes. The Press Box publishes short stories and videos produced by student journalists and school broadcast programs, with season awards for the best work. Student announcers who earn top honors call the semifinals and the championship match on Championship Saturday.

How can parents, alumni, and businesses support a school?

Anyone can give to a specific school's pickleball club through “Sponsor a High School,” support students who need help through the Every Kid Plays Fund, or join the Founding Friends. Businesses can learn about sponsorship in the Sponsors & Partners section below.

Who do I contact with questions?

Email Info@IndianaHSPickleball.org or call (317) 721-3829.

Who We Are

The Indiana Interscholastic Pickleball Association

Our mission: to foster a lifelong love of pickleball among Indiana's youth — promoting physical fitness, healthy competition, sportsmanship, scholarship, and community through the fastest-growing sport in America. We believe every Indiana student deserves access to the game, regardless of income, ability, or how they go to school — and that a sport learned at 15 can keep a Hoosier healthy at 85.

JR

J.R. Billman

Founder & CEO · Chairman of the Board

J.R. Billman is a hospital-based Clinical Staff Pharmacist in Avon, Indiana — a Hoosier born in Rochester and raised in Rushville. A past member of the Indiana State Board of Pharmacy, elected by his fellow Board members to serve as its President, he is a graduate of the University of New Mexico College of Pharmacy, where he received the Eli Lilly Award for Leadership, Scholarship, and Ethics. A former collegiate tennis player and serial entrepreneur, he picked up pickleball in November 2025 and saw what the sport was missing: the visible pathway every kid understands — a high school team to make, a state title to chase, a next level to dream about. He founded the IIPA to build that pathway and put a paddle in the hand of every Hoosier kid who wants one — and, teacher and coach by nature, to make every pickleball court a classroom where lessons bigger than the game are taught.
